REGULATORY GUIDANCE — AI SCAM SHIELD KNOWLEDGE BASE
Source: FinCEN, CFPB, Federal Reserve, NACHA, OCC
Last Updated: 2024

===== SECTION 1: FINCEN GUIDANCE ON INSTANT PAYMENT FRAUD =====

FinCEN Advisory FIN-2023-A001: Fraud Schemes Targeting Instant Payment Systems
Key Points:
- Financial institutions must implement real-time fraud detection for all instant payment transactions
- Institutions are expected to maintain velocity monitoring rules appropriate to their customer base
- Machine learning models used for fraud detection must be explainable and subject to model risk management (SR 11-7)
- Institutions must train front-line staff to recognize and respond to customer reports of instant payment fraud
- SAR filing is required for suspected fraud involving $5,000+ in losses
- Key red flags identified by FinCEN for instant payment fraud:
  a) Transactions to first-time beneficiaries above normal thresholds
  b) High velocity of outbound transactions in a short time window
  c) Newly opened accounts receiving and immediately forwarding large sums
  d) Transactions inconsistent with the customer's known profile and history
  e) Customer under unusual pressure to complete a transaction quickly

FinCEN Guidance on SARs for Instant Payment Fraud:
- Use SAR field "Structuring" for velocity-based patterns
- Use SAR field "Money laundering" for mule account patterns
- Use "Other" with narrative for APP scam / romance scam
- Include in SAR narrative: transaction IDs, amounts, dates, beneficiary account details, and the specific fraud indicators observed

===== SECTION 2: CFPB REGULATIONS ON PAYMENT FRAUD =====

Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) / Regulation E:
- Consumers have the right to dispute unauthorized electronic fund transfers
- For instant payments, the bank must investigate disputes within 10 business days (or provisionally credit within 5 business days)
- CRITICAL DISTINCTION: Authorized Push Payment (APP) scams — where the victim themselves authorized the payment — are NOT covered by Regulation E
  - Consumers bear the loss in APP scams unless the bank failed to implement adequate fraud prevention
- CFPB is actively considering extending Reg E protections to APP scams (proposed rule under review in 2024)

CFPB Supervisory Guidance on Fraud Prevention:
- Banks that do not implement "commercially reasonable" fraud prevention for instant payments may face UDAAP enforcement actions
- "Commercially reasonable" now includes: real-time risk scoring, new beneficiary friction, velocity monitoring
- Banks must clearly disclose to customers the risks of instant payments (irrevocability, speed)

===== SECTION 3: NACHA / ACH NETWORK RULES (APPLICABLE TO RTP) =====

NACHA Risk Management Framework:
- Financial institutions must maintain a fraud risk management program appropriate to the volume and risk profile of their payment activity
- Originating Depository Financial Institutions (ODFIs) bear primary liability for fraudulent ACH/RTP transactions they originate
- Return Rate Thresholds: Unauthorized return rate > 0.5% triggers NACHA review; > 1.5% results in suspension from ACH network
- Account Validation: ODFIs are required to validate new accounts before initiating first transfer (per NACHA 2022 rule)

===== SECTION 4: OCC GUIDANCE ON MODEL RISK MANAGEMENT (SR 11-7) =====

For AI/ML models used in fraud detection:
- Model Validation: All models must be independently validated before deployment and annually thereafter
- Documentation: Full documentation of model purpose, methodology, inputs, outputs, and limitations is required
- Override Tracking: All human overrides of model decisions must be logged and reviewed quarterly
- Bias Testing: Models must be tested for demographic bias; disparate impact analysis required annually
- Performance Monitoring: Key metrics (precision, recall, false positive rate) must be tracked monthly
- Model Inventory: All fraud models must be registered in the institution's model inventory

Explainability Requirements (SR 11-7 Application to Instant Payments):
- For any adverse action taken on a payment based on an AI model, the institution must be able to provide a clear, human-understandable explanation
- "Black box" decisions are not acceptable for regulatory purposes
- Explainability must be at the transaction level, not just the model level

===== SECTION 5: BANK SECRECY ACT (BSA) REQUIREMENTS =====

Customer Due Diligence (CDD) Rule:
- Financial institutions must collect and verify beneficial ownership information for legal entity customers
- Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) required for high-risk customers (PEPs, correspondent banks, etc.)
- Ongoing monitoring of customer activity against their risk profile is required

Transaction Monitoring Program Requirements:
- Must be risk-based and appropriate to the institution's size and business model
- Must include automated alerts for suspicious activity
- Must have written policies and procedures
- Must be reviewed and updated at least annually
- Staff responsible for AML/CFT must be trained at least annually

===== SECTION 6: STATE-LEVEL REGULATIONS =====

New York Department of Financial Services (NYDFS) Part 504:
- Transaction monitoring programs must be maintained in compliance with applicable AML laws
- Annual board-level certification of compliance required
- Automated transaction monitoring systems must be tested at least annually

California Consumer Financial Protection Law (CCFPL):
- Extended consumer protection rights for certain payment fraud scenarios
- Financial institutions must provide clearer disclosures about instant payment risks
- Dispute resolution timelines similar to federal Reg E requirements

===== SECTION 7: INTERNATIONAL CONSIDERATIONS =====

SWIFT CATO (Customer Security Programme):
- Mandatory controls for financial institutions using SWIFT for cross-border payments include real-time fraud monitoring

EU Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2):
- Strong Customer Authentication (SCA) required for electronic payments in EU
- Outbound payments over €30 require second-factor authentication
- Instant payment fraud liability: bank bears liability for unauthorized transactions if SCA was not applied

FATF (Financial Action Task Force) Recommendations:
- Recommendation 16 (Wire Transfer Rules): Financial institutions must obtain and transmit originator and beneficiary information with all wire transfers
- This applies to instant payments and creates a record trail essential for fraud investigation
